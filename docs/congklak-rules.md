# Congklak: Complete Rules and Playing Guide

**A traditional Indonesian two-player seed-sowing game**  
**Also known as:** *dakon*, *dhakon*, *congkak* (names vary by region)  
**Document type:** Detailed, self-contained rules reference  
**Edition:** Seven-pit, relay-sowing ruleset with documented variations  
**Last prepared:** 12 November 2026

> **Important note on regional rules:** Congklak does not have one universally binding rules authority. Indonesian families, schools, clubs, and regional traditions may differ on which player starts, whether both players begin simultaneously, how a capture works, and when or how a new round starts. **Sections 1–12 define one complete, internally consistent ruleset** you can play immediately. **Section 13 identifies alternative customs** so you can agree on them before playing. Do not mix two incompatible versions unintentionally.

## 1. At a glance

| Feature | Standard version used in this guide |
|---|---|
| Players | Exactly 2 |
| Board | 14 small pits (7 per player) and 2 large scoring stores |
| Seeds | 98 total: 7 in each small pit at the start |
| Stores at setup | Empty |
| First player | Chosen by agreement or a simple draw (*suit*) |
| Direction | Clockwise around the board, **toward your own store** along your own row; skip the opponent's store |
| Core action | Pick up all seeds from one of your occupied small pits and distribute them one by one |
| Relay sowing | If the final seed lands in a previously occupied small pit, pick up the resulting contents and keep sowing |
| Bonus turn | If the final seed lands in your own store |
| Capture (*tembak*) | If the final seed lands in an empty pit on your side and the opposite pit contains seeds |
| End of one game | Once a player's seven small pits are empty after a completed turn |
| Winner | Player with more seeds in their store after the remaining seeds are collected |

The 7-pit / 98-seed setup is widely described in Indonesian instructional material, including a publication from Indonesia's Ministry of Education and Culture.[1]

## 2. What Congklak is

Congklak is an Indonesian member of the wider **mancala family** of board games. Mancala refers to a family of games involving moving counters among pits, **not** a single game with identical rules everywhere. Congklak is played by two people facing each other across an elongated board. They distribute small seeds, shells, pebbles, or similar counters around the pits and try to accumulate the greatest number in their own large scoring pit.

In Java, related versions are often called *dakon* or *dhakon*. The word *congkak* is also used in parts of Sumatra and the broader Malay-speaking world. Names, boards, and customary rules can differ by locality.[1]

Despite its simple physical equipment, Congklak rewards counting, anticipation, timing, and planning. A single chosen pit can produce several sowing sequences, a bonus turn, or a substantial capture.

## 3. Equipment and board

You need:

- **One Congklak board** with **two parallel rows of seven small pits** and **one large store at each end** (16 pits in total).
- **98 small counters**: traditionally seeds, shells, or small stones; identical beads also work.
- **Two players**, seated on opposite sides of the board.

Each player owns:

- The **seven small pits directly in front of them**; and
- The **large store to their left from their own seated perspective** in the orientation used below.

The large scoring pit is called a **store**, **home**, **house**, *rumah*, or *lumbung*. The small pits may be called **houses**, **fields**, or *lubang kecil*, depending on the speaker and local tradition.

> **For improvised play:** Draw 14 small circles in two rows and one large circle at either end on paper or cardboard. Use 98 dry beans, stones, or counters. If playing with young children, use counters large enough to avoid choking hazards.

## 4. Board diagram and direction

This diagram is viewed from above. **Player A sits at the bottom. Player B sits at the top.** The pits with the same number face each other. Both players sow clockwise along the indicated ring. Because the players face opposite directions, each player's own store is on their **left**.

```text
                        PLAYER B
                  sow this row  --->

                 B's seven small pits
          +----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
          | B1 | B2 | B3 | B4 | B5 | B6 | B7 |       [ B STORE ]
[ A STORE ]                                   
          | A1 | A2 | A3 | A4 | A5 | A6 | A7 |
          +----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
                 A's seven small pits
                  <--- sow this row
                        PLAYER A
```

**Labels:** A1–A7 are Player A's pits; B1–B7 are Player B's pits. A1 and B1 are the leftmost opposing pair in this diagram; A7 and B7 are the rightmost opposing pair.

For complete precision, follow these paths:

- **Player A:** `A7 → A6 → A5 → A4 → A3 → A2 → A1 → A STORE → B1 → B2 → B3 → B4 → B5 → B6 → B7 → A7 → ...`
- **Player B:** `B1 → B2 → B3 → B4 → B5 → B6 → B7 → B STORE → A7 → A6 → A5 → A4 → A3 → A2 → A1 → B1 → ...`

**Both players always skip their opponent's store.** That is why A's path jumps from B7 back to A7, while B's path jumps from A1 back to B1. The arrows and paths—not the physical location of the board on the table—are the definitive movement instructions for this guide.

Note that some photographs and instructional diagrams mirror the board. Mirroring is harmless so long as the agreed sowing direction and store ownership remain consistent.

## 5. Game objective

Your goal is to place **more seeds into your own store** than your opponent places into theirs.

- Every seed in your store counts as **one point**.
- A seed entering a store **stays there** for the rest of that game.
- Seeds still in the small pits are **not yet scored**, except when collected during a capture or the end-of-game sweep.
- Since the initial game contains 98 seeds, scores after all remaining seeds are collected must sum to **98**.

This ruleset allows a **tie**, including a final score of 49–49.

## 6. Setup

1. Place the board between the two players so that each person has seven small pits directly in front of them.
2. Agree which end store belongs to each player. Under this guide's diagram, Player A uses the left store and Player B the right store, viewed from above.
3. Put **7 seeds into each of the 14 small pits**: `14 × 7 = 98` seeds total.
4. Leave both large stores **empty**.
5. Decide who goes first—for example by *suit* (a simple hand-game draw), a coin toss, or mutual agreement.
6. The selected player begins. **Players take turns, rather than starting simultaneously**, in this main ruleset.

**Initial count:**

```text
B STORE: 0   B1 B2 B3 B4 B5 B6 B7: 7 7 7 7 7 7 7
A STORE: 0   A1 A2 A3 A4 A5 A6 A7: 7 7 7 7 7 7 7
```

## 7. Legal moves and sowing

### 7.1 Choosing a pit

On your turn, choose **one small pit on your own side** containing at least one seed. You may not begin a move from an opponent's pit, a store, or an empty pit.

### 7.2 Picking up seeds

Lift **all** seeds from your selected pit into your hand. The chosen pit is now empty. You cannot choose to leave some of its seeds behind.

### 7.3 Distributing seeds (*menabur*)

Starting with the **next** pit along your sowing path, drop **exactly one** seed in each successive eligible pit until your hand is empty.

- Include all small pits on both players' sides as you encounter them.
- Include **your own store** when your sowing path reaches it.
- **Do not put a seed in your opponent's store**: jump over it.
- Do not drop a seed into the starting pit immediately on pickup; it receives a seed only if the ongoing distribution comes around to it again.
- Keep the same movement direction throughout the entire turn, including after a relay sow.

### 7.4 What happens after sowing the last seed?

Check **the exact destination of the last seed** and follow Section 8. A turn may contain **many relay sowings** before it finally ends.

### 7.5 What counts as one turn?

A turn consists of the initial sowing plus every required relay sowing that follows. It ends only when the player:

- Lands the last seed in a previously empty small pit (with capture where permitted); or
- Completes a sowing in their own store, receives a bonus turn, and then eventually finishes that bonus turn; or
- Reaches the end-of-game condition.

**For clarity:** Landing in your own store grants a **fresh choice of your own occupied pit**. It is convenient to call that choice a *bonus turn* even though the same person keeps playing.

## 8. Where the last seed lands

Every sowing ends in exactly one of these four situations. **"Empty" and "occupied" refer to the small pit's contents immediately *before* you drop the final seed into it.**

| Last-seed destination | Result |
|---|---|
| A **small pit that already held one or more seeds** | **Relay sow:** pick up all seeds now in that pit, including the seed just added, and continue distributing from the *next* pit. This applies on either player's side. |
| **Your own large store** | **Bonus turn:** leave that seed in your store. Choose any occupied small pit on your side and start a new sowing. |
| A **previously empty small pit on your side** | The sowing stops. Check for a **capture** (*tembak*) using Section 9; whether a capture occurs or not, your turn ends. |
| A **previously empty small pit on the opponent's side** | Leave your final seed in that pit. Your turn ends; the opponent plays next. No capture. |

Because an opponent's store is always skipped, it cannot be the final-seed destination.

### 8.1 Relay sowing in detail

Suppose the last seed lands in a small pit that contained 3 seeds just before you placed it. That pit now contains 4 seeds. You immediately pick up **all 4** and distribute them one by one starting at the next pit in your sowing path.

- You **do not** place these 4 directly into your store.
- You **do not** choose a different pit for this relay; you must continue from the pit where the seed landed.
- This may repeat many times. Only the **last** pit reached by each hand of seeds determines whether the turn continues.

### 8.2 Bonus turns in detail

If the last seed falls into **your own** store:

1. Keep that seed in your store.
2. Look at your own seven small pits.
3. Choose **any occupied** pit among them.
4. Pick up all its seeds and start a new sowing along your normal path.

You can earn multiple bonus turns in succession. If no pit on your side contains seeds, apply the end-of-game rule in Section 10 rather than attempting an illegal move.

## 9. Capturing: *tembak*

A capture, traditionally called **menembak** or **tembak**, rewards a move that finishes in an empty pit on your side opposite a pit containing your opponent's seeds.

### 9.1 Capture requirements

All of the following must be true:

1. You have just distributed the **last seed in your hand**.
2. That seed landed in a **small pit on your own side**.
3. That pit was **empty immediately before** the last seed landed in it.
4. The **directly opposite small pit** on your opponent's side contains **at least one seed**.

### 9.2 Capture procedure in this edition

- Take the **one last seed** just placed into your empty pit.
- Take **every seed** from the directly opposite opponent pit.
- Put **all those seeds** into **your own store**.
- Leave both affected small pits empty.
- **End your turn.** The opponent plays next, unless the game has ended.

**Example:** Player A's `A3` is empty, `B3` contains 5, and A finishes by placing one last seed into `A3`. A moves **6 seeds** to A's store: **5 from B3 + the 1 final seed from A3**.

### 9.3 If the opposite pit is empty

If your final seed lands in an empty pit on your side but the opposite pit is **also empty**, there is **no capture**. In this edition, the final seed **stays in your small pit**, and your turn ends.

### 9.4 Capture limitations

- You cannot capture when you stop in an empty pit **on your opponent's side**.
- You cannot capture merely because you **pass through** an empty pit during a longer sowing.
- You cannot capture from a non-opposite pit; capture is from the **directly facing** pit only.
- You cannot capture after landing in your own store; that event gives a bonus turn instead.

**Variation alert:** In some Indonesian traditions, only the opponent's seeds move to the store and your final seed remains in its small pit. Some other traditions require a full circuit of the board before a capture is valid. Both are discussed in Section 13; **neither restriction applies to the default ruleset here**.[2][3][4]

## 10. Ending the game and scoring

### 10.1 When the game ends

Under this single-game edition, the game ends **after the currently active turn is resolved** if either player has **no seeds in any of their seven small pits**. This avoids ending the game halfway through a player's hand of seeds.

Equivalently: when a player cannot make a legal choice from their own row and the current sowing has finished, there is no further ordinary move to play.

### 10.2 Collect remaining seeds (the sweep)

If one side is empty and the other side still has seeds, the player with remaining seeds **takes all seeds left in their own seven small pits and puts them into their own store**.

In the unusual event both rows are already empty, there is nothing to sweep.

### 10.3 Determine the winner

1. Count all seeds in Player A's store.
2. Count all seeds in Player B's store.
3. The higher count wins.
4. Equal counts mean a **draw**.

**Score example:** A has 61 seeds and B has 37; `61 + 37 = 98`, so A wins by 24 seeds.

**Sanity check:** If the two final counts do not add to 98 in the seven-pit setup, search for a misplaced or lost counter before declaring a result.

> **Not the same as every traditional multi-round game:** Some households distinguish a round ending when one player runs out of playable seeds (*menang jalan*) from ultimately winning by seeds (*menang biji*), then redistribute the seeds for another round. See Section 13 for that style.

## 11. Worked examples

These examples use the board numbering in Section 4. Where they describe a position other than the start, assume it arose during an ongoing game. Counts in unmentioned pits are irrelevant to the specific example.

### Example 1: A guaranteed bonus turn from the starting position

From the original setup, `A7` contains exactly **7** seeds. Player A chooses `A7` and distributes one seed to each next destination:

```text
Pick up: A7 (7 seeds)
Drop 1: A6
Drop 2: A5
Drop 3: A4
Drop 4: A3
Drop 5: A2
Drop 6: A1
Drop 7: A STORE
```

The final seed lands in A's store, so **A earns another turn** and may select any nonempty A pit. A's store now contains at least 1 seed.

**Key lesson:** Count how many sowing steps it takes to reach your store.

### Example 2: Relay sowing, followed by an empty opponent pit

Suppose:

- `A3` has **2** seeds.
- `A2` has **4** seeds.
- `A1` has **3** seeds.
- `B3` is **empty**.

Player A chooses `A3`:

1. Take 2 seeds from `A3`.
2. Drop 1 into `A2` (which now has 5).
3. Drop 1 into `A1` (which now has 4). This was the **last** seed, and A1 was **already occupied**.
4. Immediately pick up **all 4 seeds** from `A1` and continue.
5. Drop those 4 into `A STORE`, `B1`, `B2`, and `B3`, in that order.
6. The fourth and final seed goes into previously empty `B3`, on the **opponent's side**.
7. The turn **ends without capture**. The final seed remains in `B3`.

**Key lesson:** A relay is mandatory, and the last seed's destination after the relay determines the outcome.

### Example 3: Capturing six seeds

Suppose:

- `A4` contains **1** seed.
- `A3` is **empty**.
- `B3` contains **5** seeds.

A chooses `A4`, takes its one seed, and drops it into `A3`. Since `A3` was empty and is on A's side, A checks the facing pit `B3`. It contains 5, so A captures:

```text
A3 final seed              1
Opposite B3 seeds          5
                           -
Seeds added to A STORE     6
```

`A3` and `B3` are now empty. **A's turn is over.**

### Example 4: An empty pit with no capture

A's last seed lands in previously empty `A2`, but `B2` is empty as well. Leave the final seed in `A2`; move no seeds to either store; end A's turn. B now plays.

### Example 5: Reaching the final score

After B finishes a turn, all B small pits are empty. A still has 9 seeds scattered among A's small pits. Under the default end rule, A immediately moves those **9** remaining seeds into A's store, and both stores are counted. No additional sowing turns occur.

## 12. Rules clarifications and unusual cases

**Can I pick up seeds from an opponent's small pit?**  
Not as the initial move or when starting a bonus turn. **Yes during a relay**: if your last seed falls into an already occupied small pit on the opponent's side, you must pick up the resulting contents and continue sowing.

**Do I put seeds into my opponent's small pits?**  
Yes. All small pits on both rows participate in ordinary sowing. Only the opponent's **large store** is skipped.

**May I start from an empty pit?**  
No. A legal starting pit must be on your side and contain at least one seed.

**What if my original pit is reached again?**  
It receives seeds like any other small pit. The fact that you originally picked seeds from it does not block it.

**If my last seed falls in a small pit that had exactly one seed, do I relay?**  
Yes. It now contains two; pick up both and continue.

**Does a relay happen if the last seed lands in my own store and the store already had seeds?**  
No. Stores are not ordinary small pits. Keep the seed there and take a **bonus turn** instead.

**Can I choose any pit after a bonus turn?**  
Any **occupied small pit on your own side**, yes. Not an opponent's pit, an empty pit, or a store.

**Can I capture if the empty pit is on the opponent's side?**  
No. That simply ends your turn.

**Can I capture seeds from a pit next to the opposite pit?**  
No. Only the directly facing pit counts.

**Do I have to capture when eligible?**  
Under this edition, **yes**. Capturing is mandatory and immediately ends your turn. Some informal games treat capturing as optional; agree before starting if you prefer that.

**May seeds already scored in a store be used again?**  
Not during the **same game**. A multi-round carryover variant may redistribute them between rounds; see Section 13.

**Can a player pass voluntarily?**  
No. If you have at least one occupied small pit and it is your turn, you must make a legal move.

**Can I count seeds before choosing?**  
Yes under this guide. All small-pit contents are public information. In informal play you may agree on a time limit for thinking.

**What if counters are spilled or a sowing error is noticed?**  
If both players know the last valid position, restore it and replay the move. If not, agree to reconstruct it, restart, or adjudicate fairly. These are suggested etiquette rules, not a universal historical custom.

**How many seeds should be on the board at any moment?**  
Always **98 total** across both stores, both rows, and any seed temporarily in a player's hand. Sowing and capturing do not create or destroy seeds.

## 13. Regional and house-rule variations

**Use these as alternatives, not as additional rules that automatically apply.** Before play, agree on the items that can change who wins.

### 13.1 Who starts?

**Default in this document:** One player wins a draw and begins; the other waits.  
**Documented alternative:** Some local games have both players start sowing simultaneously. This is mentioned by Indonesian guides and local descriptions of *dakon/congklak*.[2][5]

If using a simultaneous start, agree on how to resolve simultaneous access to the same pit and when to switch to alternating play. A straightforward formal game is easier to administer with sequential turns.

### 13.2 Direction and board orientation

**Default:** Use the paths in Section 4, going toward your own store on your own side and skipping your opponent's store. Indonesian instructional texts commonly describe sowing *searah jarum jam* (clockwise).[1][5]

**Alternative:** Some relatives or local variants may orient the board differently or use a different stated direction. The essential point is to agree on an explicit pit-by-pit sequence rather than rely on the word “clockwise” alone.

### 13.3 What exactly is taken in a capture?

**Default:** Capture **the final seed plus all seeds opposite**. This is the explicit rule used in one published Congklak rules reference.[3]

**Alternative:** Capture **only the opponent's opposite seeds**, leaving the final seed in your own pit. Another Indonesian instructional guide notes that both practices exist, while Indonesian local-government descriptions often state that the seeds in the opposite pit are taken without specifying the fate of the final seed.[2][5]

Decide beforehand which treatment you use. Both preserve the total seed count; they change the distribution of points and playable seeds.

### 13.4 Must you complete a full circuit before capturing?

**Default:** No full circuit is required. An eligible landing in your own empty pit may capture whether it occurred early in a sowing or after several circuits.[3]

**Alternative:** Some traditions require the move to have gone around the board at least once before a shot (*tembak*) is allowed.[4] If adopting this variant, agree exactly how to track completed circuits during relay sowing.

### 13.5 When does a round end?

**Default single-game finish:** Once a completed turn leaves a player with no seeds in their row, sweep any remaining seeds to the other player's store and score the game.[3]

**Alternatives:** Some traditions treat this as the end of a **round** rather than the entire match, and may use *menang jalan* (roughly, winning by continuing to move) and *menang biji* (winning by accumulated seeds) as distinct outcomes.[4] Other teaching descriptions speak of play ending when all small pits are empty.[2]

Do not automatically apply the default sweep to a multi-round format whose traditions require those seeds to be retained and redistributed instead.

### 13.6 Traditional-style multi-round play (*ngacang* and protected or inactive houses)

A longer form can carry forward the seeds won in one round into the next. Published descriptions differ significantly; **the following is an overview, not a claim that every Indonesian community follows identical rules**.

A documented Indonesian-style *ngacang* approach includes these ideas:[4]

1. After a round, players take seeds from their own stores and any seeds remaining on their own side.
2. They try to refill their own small pits to **7 seeds each**, starting near their own store.
3. A player with surplus seeds puts the excess into their store.
4. A player with fewer than 49 seeds cannot fully refill every pit and may have special underfilled or empty pits called *ngacang*.
5. These special pits may be **protected**: an opponent skips them when sowing and cannot capture their contents; the owner may be allowed to sow into them. The exact treatment varies.
6. The next round continues under the local rules for protected pits, starting player, and overall victory.

A distinct **burned/inactive-house** tradition regards some unrefillable pits as unusable in later rounds. Such houses may not be selected or sown into.[6] This is **not identical** to every version of *ngacang*.

**Practical recommendation:** For a first game or a classroom demonstration, play Sections 1–12 as one complete game. Introduce multi-round *ngacang* only if both players have an agreed reference or a familiar local custom that specifies exactly how special pits function.

### 13.7 Smaller or larger boards

Boards with a different number of small pits exist. For casual adaptation, one simple convention is to put as many seeds in each small pit as the number of small pits in each row: for example, a five-pit board would start with `2 × 5 × 5 = 50` seeds. This is a **setup convention**, not a universal requirement; always agree on the initial number and the rules for captures and stores.

### 13.8 Tournament or online adaptations

Some apps or organized play formats add match points, fixed thinking times, or predetermined sets of games. These are **competition formats**, not basic physical rules of Congklak. For example, an online rules reference defines a separate 2-point win / 1-point draw / 0-point loss system for multi-game matches.[3]

## 14. Playing multiple games and tournaments

For a simple social match that avoids protected-pit complications, play a series of **fresh, independent games**, each reset to 7 seeds in all 14 small pits:

- **Best of three:** First player to win two games wins the match; if games can draw, agree how ties affect the count.
- **Three-game points format:** Award **2 match points for a win, 1 each for a draw, 0 for a loss**. Higher total wins.
- **Alternating starts:** Let players alternate the first move between games, or let the previous loser start, as agreed.

These are recommended formats for convenience and fairness, **not claimed as universal traditional rules**.

For organized play, record the following in advance: sowing path; capture method; any full-circuit requirement; starting-player rule; end-of-round rule; match length; permitted thinking time; and handling of illegal moves.

## 15. Strategy and beginner advice

1. **Look for store finishes.** Calculate how many steps it will take the last seed to reach your own store. Bonus turns can be very valuable.
2. **Plan relays, not just the first handful.** A landing in a populated small pit forces you to keep moving, sometimes far beyond the apparent destination of your initial seeds.
3. **Watch the opposing pits.** An empty pit on your side directly opposite a well-stocked enemy pit can become a powerful capture opportunity.
4. **Do not expose large captures.** If your opponent can finish in an empty pit opposite one of your seed-heavy pits, that stockpile is at risk.
5. **Understand the endgame.** In the default edition, any seeds left on a side when the opposite row is emptied will be swept to their owner's store. Emptying a row is not always equivalent to winning the score.
6. **Keep a running store count.** Stores are already scored; seeds in small pits remain in circulation.
7. **Think beyond one move.** When choosing between two legal starts, compare their likely relay endings, captures, and bonus-turn possibilities.

**Beginner exercise:** From a fresh starting board, have Player A find a move that places its seventh seed directly into A's store. Then replay with a different starting pit and predict whether the last seed triggers a relay.

## 17. Quick-reference sheet

```text
CONGKLAK — ONE-GAME RULES (7 SMALL PITS EACH)

SETUP
  2 players; 14 small pits; 2 large stores.
  Put 7 seeds in EACH small pit = 98 total.
  Leave stores empty. Decide who begins.

YOUR TURN
  1. Pick any OCCUPIED small pit on YOUR row.
  2. Pick up ALL its seeds.
  3. Sow 1 per next pit, following the agreed path.
  4. Include YOUR store; SKIP opponent's store.

WHEN YOUR LAST SEED LANDS...
  - In an OCCUPIED SMALL PIT:
      Pick up all seeds now there; keep sowing.
  - In YOUR STORE:
      Bonus turn; choose any occupied pit on your row.
  - In an EMPTY PIT ON YOUR ROW:
      If opposite pit has seeds, capture its seeds
      AND your final seed into your store.
      Otherwise leave your final seed there.
      Either way, your turn ends.
  - In an EMPTY PIT ON OPPONENT'S ROW:
      Leave the seed there; your turn ends.

FINISH
  After a completed turn, if either row is empty:
  sweep seeds remaining on the other row into that
  player's store. Count stores. Most seeds wins.
  A tie is possible. Final store counts add to 98.

BEFORE PLAYING A LOCAL VARIANT, AGREE ON:
  capture method, full-circuit condition, whether
  starts are simultaneous, and multi-round rules.
```

This guide is a **synthesis**, not a claim of a single official nationwide rulebook. Sources support both the shared mechanics and the fact that household/region-specific differences exist.

1. **Indonesian Ministry of Education and Culture, Language Development and Fostering Agency (2018).** Andreas Supriyono, *Serunya Permainan Tradisional Anak Zaman Dulu*, pp. 21–24, section “Congklak.” Covers the 16-hole/98-seed setup, first-player draw, sowing, relay, stores, and scoring. [Read the government-hosted PDF](https://badanbahasa.kemendikdasmen.go.id/resource/doc/files/Serunya_Permainan_Tradisional_Anak_Zaman_Dulu_0.pdf).
2. **IndoBoardGames, “Cara main Congklak.”** Indonesian guide explaining sowing, store skips, relay sowing, captures, and some house-rule differences. [Read the guide](https://indoboardgames.com/cara-main/congklak).
3. **Congklak.com, “How to Play Congklak.”** A specific, self-contained ruleset including captures of both the final seed and opposing seeds, the one-game sweep, and optional match scoring. [Read the rules](https://congklak.com/en/rules/).
4. **Expat.or.id, “Instructions for How to Play Congklak.”** Describes a multi-round Indonesian-style game including *menang jalan*, *menang biji*, *tembak*, and *ngacang*. Its details are **not identical** to all other sources. [Read the instructions](https://www.expat.or.id/info/congklakinstructions.html).
5. **Kalurahan Donoharjo, Sleman, “Dakon / Congklak.”** Local-government description including simultaneous starts, clockwise sowing, relay sowing, and *menembak*. [Read the local description](https://donoharjosid.slemankab.go.id/home/data-kebudayaan/dakon-congklak/).
6. **Jabatan Kebudayaan dan Kesenian Negara (Malaysia), “Congkak.”** Comparative regional reference discussing captures, opening practices, and later rounds with unavailable (“burned”) houses. Relevant to distinguishing neighboring rules traditions, but **not evidence that every Indonesian version uses those rules**. [Read the cultural reference](https://pemetaanbudaya.jkkn.gov.my/en/senibudaya/detail/1344).
